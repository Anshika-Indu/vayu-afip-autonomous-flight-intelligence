# AFIP — Information Architecture
## What the Aircraft Knows, How It Knows It, and How Knowledge Becomes Action

---

## 0. Framing

Software architecture answers "what components exist." Information architecture answers a different question: **what does the aircraft actually *know* at any given instant, where does that knowledge live, how does it change, and how does knowledge become a decision?**

This document deliberately ignores module boundaries and talks only in terms of **information objects**, **flows**, and **transformations** — the same way you'd describe the "brain" of a self-driving car or a legged robot: sensors produce *evidence*, evidence is fused into *belief*, belief drives *intent*, intent drives *action*, and action produces new evidence. AFIP is one long loop of that cycle, running at different speeds for different kinds of knowledge.

---

## 1. What Information Exists Inside the Drone

All information the aircraft holds falls into six categories. Every single data object anywhere in the system is an instance of one of these.

| Category | Nature | Example | Volatility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Raw Evidence** | Unprocessed or lightly processed sensor output | LiDAR point cloud frame, raw IMU sample, ESC temperature reading | Extremely high — refreshed every cycle, discarded after fusion |
| **Belief** | Fused, confidence-weighted interpretation of evidence | "There is an obstacle at bearing 040, 30m, 0.9 confidence" | High — continuously updated, always carries confidence + timestamp |
| **State** | The aircraft's own condition, as distinct from the world around it | Battery %, attitude, tilt-rotor angle, cargo mass | High — but bounded by physics, so more predictable than belief |
| **Intent** | What the aircraft is currently trying to do | "Transition to forward flight," "Hold current position," "Divert to alternate LZ" | Medium — changes only on deliberate decision, not every cycle |
| **Policy / Constraint** | What the aircraft is *allowed* to do, largely fixed for a given flight | Geofence boundary, structural load limit, energy reserve floor | Low — set pre-flight, rarely changes mid-flight |
| **History / Record** | What has already happened, immutable once written | Past decisions, past state, explanation records | Zero — append-only, never mutated |

Everything the aircraft "knows" is one of these six things, at one of these six volatility tiers. This taxonomy is what lets a single World Model (Section 3) hold radically different kinds of knowledge without becoming an undifferentiated blob.

## 2. How Information Flows Between "Modules" (Described as Flow, Not Components)

Ignore *what* processes information and look only at the **shape of the flow**:

```
EVIDENCE  →  BELIEF  →  STATE (self) + WORLD (environment)
                              │
                              ▼
                     EXECUTIVE EVALUATION
                              │
                 ┌────────────┼─────────────┐
                 ▼            ▼             ▼
             INTENT      CONSTRAINT      HISTORY
           (proposed)     CHECK          (logged
                 │        (accept/         regardless
                 ▼         reject/         of outcome)
           INTENT (approved)  modify)
                 │
                 ▼
            ACTUATION REQUEST
             (to flight controller)
                 │
                 ▼
          NEW EVIDENCE (the world/aircraft changed)
                 │
                 └──────────────► loop continues
```

Three properties define this flow, and they hold everywhere in the system:

1. **Evidence never skips fusion.** Nothing downstream ever reasons directly on raw sensor data — it always reasons on *belief*, which already carries a confidence value. This is what prevents a single noisy sensor reading from ever directly triggering a decision.
2. **Intent never skips constraint-checking.** No proposed intent — whether generated by routine mission logic or by an emergency response — reaches the aircraft without passing through the constraint layer. There is no "urgent bypass" path in the information flow, because bypassing constraint-checking is precisely how urgent-but-wrong decisions become incidents.
3. **History captures everything, unconditionally.** The record layer subscribes to every flow above, including rejected intents and discarded evidence-confidence collapses — the history is not just "what the aircraft did," it's "what the aircraft considered and didn't do."

## 3. The World Model — Detailed Definition

The **World Model** is the aircraft's single, authoritative, continuously-updated belief state. It is not a database in the conventional sense — it is a **versioned belief graph**: a snapshot of everything currently believed to be true, where every fact carries provenance (which evidence produced it), a confidence value, and a timestamp of last update.

**3.1 Core properties of the World Model**

- **Single writer principle per fact.** Every fact in the World Model has exactly one authoritative source of truth at any moment (e.g., aircraft position is authoritatively written by the fusion process that combines GPS+INS+vision, never independently overwritten by a second process). Multiple *contributors* can feed evidence toward a fact, but only the fusion step commits it.
- **Confidence is a property of the fact, not a separate lookup.** You cannot ask the World Model "where is the obstacle" without also getting "how sure are we" — confidence is inseparable from the value.
- **Freshness is explicit.** Every fact carries `lastUpdated`. Nothing in the World Model is silently allowed to go stale — a fact whose age exceeds its defined validity window is marked `STALE` and treated by the Executive as reduced-confidence, not as still-true.
- **Read-many, write-few.** Almost everything reads the World Model. Only the fusion/estimation processes write to it. This asymmetry is what keeps the aircraft's picture of reality coherent — there is one belief, not N slightly different beliefs held by N different consumers.
- **Layered, not flat.** The World Model is organized into distinct sub-graphs (below) so a consumer can subscribe to only the slice it needs (e.g., the Executive needs everything; the dashboard needs a curated summary; the mission-optimization reasoning needs only energy/route-relevant facts).

**3.2 Sub-graphs of the World Model**

| Sub-graph | Contains | Update source |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-State** | Position, velocity, attitude, tilt-rotor configuration, energy state, structural/vibration state, cargo mass & balance | Fusion of IMU/GPS/EKF, battery telemetry, load cells |
| **Environment** | Obstacles, terrain, weather (wind, visibility, precipitation), airspace occupancy | Fusion of LiDAR/EO-IR/radar, weather feed |
| **Traffic** | Cooperative (ADS-B/UTM) and non-cooperative contacts, their tracked trajectories and predicted paths | Fusion of traffic feed + onboard detection |
| **Mission** | Current task graph, active leg, remaining objectives, delivery status | Mission planning process, updated as tasks complete |
| **Health** | Per-subsystem status (NOMINAL/DEGRADED/CRITICAL) and trend | Health estimation process |
| **Constraint Snapshot** | The currently-active subset of policy relevant right now (e.g., which geofence cell the aircraft is in) | Derived from static policy + Self-State/Environment |

**3.3 What the World Model explicitly is *not***

- It is not a log (that's History — append-only, separate).
- It is not intent (Intent Model is separate — see Section 7 — because "what we believe" and "what we're trying to do" must never be conflated; conflating them is how systems start rationalizing beliefs to fit intentions).
- It is not a command queue.

## 4. Every Object Stored Inside the World Model

Below is the canonical object inventory. Each object is described purely as information — fields, not implementation.

**4.1 Self-State objects**

- `AircraftPose` — position (lat/lon/alt), velocity vector, attitude (roll/pitch/yaw), angular rates, confidence, timestamp
- `PropulsionState` — per-rotor RPM, tilt angle (for tilt-rotor conversion), motor temperature, vibration signature
- `EnergyState` — battery/fuel remaining, discharge rate, estimated range/endurance remaining, thermal state
- `StructuralState` — load-sensor readings, estimated stress margins, vibration trend
- `CargoState` — mass, center-of-gravity offset, restraint/lock status, shift-event flags
- `ComputeState` — companion computer health, process liveness, link quality to flight controller

**4.2 Environment objects**

- `ObstacleTrack` — one per tracked obstacle: relative position, velocity if moving, classification, confidence, source sensor(s), first-seen/last-seen timestamps
- `TerrainProfile` — local elevation model beneath current and near-future flight path
- `WeatherEstimate` — wind vector, gust estimate, visibility, precipitation state, confidence, source (onboard sensed vs. forecast-derived)
- `AirspaceOccupancy` — active restrictions, temporary flight restrictions if updated in-flight, current geofence cell

**4.3 Traffic objects**

- `TrafficContact` — one per contact: position, velocity, predicted trajectory, cooperative/non-cooperative flag, time-to-closest-approach, confidence

**4.4 Mission objects**

- `MissionObjective` — the top-level goal (deliver payload to X by time Y)
- `TaskGraphNode` — one per decomposed task: type (transit/hover/transition/deliver), preconditions, status (pending/active/complete/failed), dependencies
- `ActiveLeg` — the currently executing task, with progress fraction and expected completion

**4.5 Health objects**

- `SubsystemHealth` — one per subsystem: status enum, trend vector, time-to-threshold estimate, contributing evidence references
- `AnomalyRecord` — active anomaly: type, affected subsystem, confidence, first-detected timestamp

**4.6 Constraint Snapshot objects**

- `ActiveGeofence` — current permitted volume
- `ActiveEnergyPolicy` — current minimum-reserve requirement (may vary by mission phase)
- `ActiveStructuralLimit` — current load/stress ceiling given cargo and flight phase

Every object above carries the same three universal metadata fields: `confidence`, `lastUpdated`, `provenance` (which evidence/process produced it). This uniformity is what allows the Executive (Section 8) to reason generically about "how much do I trust this fact" without needing bespoke logic per object type.

## 5. How Sensors Update the World Model

Sensors never write to the World Model directly. Every update passes through the same three-stage transformation, regardless of sensor type:

```
STAGE 1 — INGEST
Raw sensor sample arrives with its own native timestamp and
native noise characteristics. No interpretation yet.

STAGE 2 — FUSE
Sample is combined with other evidence bearing on the same fact
(e.g., GPS + INS + visual odometry all bear on AircraftPose).
Output is a single belief value + a confidence derived from
sensor agreement, individual sensor trust weighting, and
recency.

STAGE 3 — COMMIT
The fused belief is written into the relevant World Model
object, replacing the previous value, with lastUpdated set to
now and provenance set to the contributing evidence set.
Any consumer subscribed to that object is notified of the
change.
```

**5.1 Multi-sensor agreement rules**

- **Agreement raises confidence.** When independent sensors bearing on the same fact agree within expected tolerance, confidence rises above any single sensor's baseline.
- **Disagreement doesn't average blindly — it triggers a confidence penalty.** If two sensors disagree beyond tolerance, the fused belief's confidence drops sharply rather than splitting the difference silently; a silently-averaged wrong answer is more dangerous than a flagged uncertain one.
- **Single-sensor facts are capped.** Any fact currently supported by only one sensor (redundant sensor down/degraded) has its confidence ceiling capped below what full-redundancy would allow, regardless of how "clean" that single reading looks.

**5.2 Update cadence is fact-dependent, not sensor-dependent**

Different World Model objects tolerate different staleness:

| Object class | Typical update rate | Staleness threshold |
|---|---|---|
| `AircraftPose`, `PropulsionState` | Tens of Hz | Sub-second |
| `ObstacleTrack` (near-field) | ~10–20 Hz | ~1 second |
| `WeatherEstimate`, `AirspaceOccupancy` | ~0.1–1 Hz | Tens of seconds to minutes |
| `SubsystemHealth` trend | ~1 Hz | Few seconds |
| `MissionObjective`, `TaskGraphNode` | Event-driven only | N/A — updates on task completion, not time |

This is why the World Model is described as layered rather than flat: a single global "refresh rate" would either starve fast-changing facts or flood slow-changing ones with redundant updates.

## 6. How Every Decision Is Made From the World Model

No decision-making process ever consults raw sensors, mission files, or health telemetry directly — every decision reads exclusively from the World Model. This is the single most important information-architecture rule in the whole system, because it guarantees that *whatever* is making the decision (rule-based logic or a learned model) is reasoning over the same consistent, confidence-scored picture as everything else.

**6.1 The generic decision-read pattern**

Every decision cycle follows the same read pattern regardless of what specific decision is being made:

1. Read relevant World Model sub-graph(s) (e.g., a routing decision reads Self-State + Environment + Mission; a health-driven abort decision reads Self-State + Health).
2. Check freshness of every fact read — any `STALE` fact is treated as reduced-confidence input, not full-confidence input.
3. Weight the decision by aggregate confidence — a decision made on low-confidence inputs is itself tagged low-confidence, which downstream constraint-checking treats more conservatively.
4. Produce a proposed **Intent** (never a raw command — see Section 7).
5. Reference exactly which World Model facts (with their confidence/timestamp) justified the proposed Intent — this reference set becomes the explanation trace, generated as a byproduct of the read pattern rather than reconstructed afterward.

**6.2 Decision tiers by information scope**

| Decision tier | World Model scope read | Typical cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Reflexive avoidance (imminent obstacle/traffic) | Self-State + near-field Environment + Traffic only | Fastest cycle |
| Tactical (route/altitude/speed adjustment) | Self-State + Environment + Health | Fast cycle |
| Strategic (mission re-plan, divert, abort) | Full World Model | Medium cycle |
| Optimization (efficiency improvements) | Mission + Self-State (energy) + Weather | Slowest cycle, always advisory |

Narrower-scope, faster-cycle decisions are structurally incapable of being overridden by slower strategic reasoning arriving late — the reflexive tier acts on the freshest near-field facts without waiting for a strategic-tier cycle to complete, which is what keeps collision avoidance from ever queuing behind a mission optimization computation.

## 7. The Intent Model

The **Intent Model** is a distinct information structure from the World Model — it represents *what the aircraft is trying to do*, never *what the aircraft believes to be true*. Keeping these separate prevents a subtle but serious failure mode: a system that lets "desired state" leak into "believed state" will start believing what it wants to be true.

**7.1 Structure of an Intent object**

Every Intent, at any level of the system, has the same shape:

- `intentType` — category (e.g., TRANSIT, HOLD, TRANSITION, DIVERT, ABORT, DELIVER)
- `targetState` — the Self-State/Mission values this intent aims to achieve (e.g., target position, target task completion)
- `justification` — reference to the specific World Model facts (with confidence/timestamp) that produced this intent
- `priority` — where this intent sits in the priority hierarchy (reflexive > tactical > strategic > optimization)
- `status` — PROPOSED → CONSTRAINT-CHECKED → APPROVED → ACTIVE → COMPLETE/SUPERSEDED/REJECTED
- `supersedes` — reference to any prior intent this one replaces, preserving the chain

**7.2 Intent lifecycle**

```
PROPOSED  →  CONSTRAINT-CHECKED  →  APPROVED  →  ACTIVE  →  COMPLETE
    │                │                              │
    │                └── REJECTED (with reason,     └── SUPERSEDED
    │                    logged, does not             (higher-priority
    │                    reach actuation)              intent preempts)
    └── (multiple proposals can compete; only one
         per priority tier can be ACTIVE at a time)
```

**7.3 Intent hierarchy resolves conflict by priority, not by recency**

If a reflexive-tier intent (avoid imminent obstacle) and a strategic-tier intent (continue mission leg) are simultaneously active, the reflexive intent always takes precedence — priority tier is fixed by *category*, not by which intent was generated most recently. This prevents a slow strategic re-plan from ever "talking the aircraft out of" an urgent avoidance maneuver simply by arriving a moment later.

## 8. The Executive (Decision Engine) Logic — as Information Transformation

Described purely in information terms, the Executive is the single transformation point where **World Model + active Intent + Constraint Snapshot → next Intent (or confirmation of current Intent)**.

**8.1 The Executive's information contract**

- **Reads:** full World Model (scoped per decision tier, Section 6.2), currently ACTIVE Intent, current Constraint Snapshot.
- **Writes:** a new or confirmed Intent (with `status: PROPOSED`), and a Decision Trace (justification reference set).
- **Never writes:** World Model facts (it consumes belief, it never fabricates it) or actuation commands directly (it produces Intent; a separate constraint-check/translation step turns Intent into actuation — the Executive's output is always one level removed from the aircraft's actuators).

**8.2 Executive evaluation logic, as an information cascade**

```
1. Is any required World Model fact STALE or below minimum
   confidence for this decision tier?
      → YES: propose conservative-fallback Intent (e.g., HOLD),
        justification = "insufficient confidence in <fact>"

2. Does any Health or Anomaly fact exceed the CRITICAL threshold?
      → YES: propose ABORT-class Intent, justification = specific
        SubsystemHealth/AnomalyRecord references

3. Does any Health or Anomaly fact exceed the DEGRADED threshold?
      → YES: propose CONSERVATIVE-class Intent (reduced envelope),
        justification = specific facts

4. None of the above:
      → propose CONTINUE Intent (next TaskGraphNode from Mission
        sub-graph), justification = current ActiveLeg progress
```

Every branch of this cascade produces an Intent with an attached justification — there is no branch that produces a bare, unexplained decision. This is what makes the Explainability capability structural rather than bolted on: the justification isn't generated after the decision, it's produced *as* the decision.

**8.3 Constraint-check is a separate information transformation**

The Executive's output (`Intent, status: PROPOSED`) passes to a constraint-check transformation that reads the Constraint Snapshot and either:
- confirms the Intent unchanged (`status: APPROVED`),
- modifies it to fit within constraints (e.g., caps a speed value) and marks it `MODIFIED`, or
- rejects it outright (`status: REJECTED`) with a constraint reference.

Only an `APPROVED` (including `MODIFIED→APPROVED`) Intent ever proceeds to actuation translation.

## 9. Complete Event-Driven Architecture

Two distinct information regimes coexist: **continuous belief update** (Sections 5–6, running on fixed cadences) and **discrete event propagation** (this section). Events are the mechanism by which a change in one part of the information graph immediately triggers evaluation elsewhere, without waiting for the next scheduled cycle.

**9.1 Event categories**

| Event class | Triggered by | Propagates to |
|---|---|---|
| `FactUpdated` | Any World Model object commit (Stage 3, Section 5) | All subscribers of that object/sub-graph |
| `ConfidenceCollapsed` | A fact's confidence drops below its decision-relevant threshold | Executive (immediate re-evaluation, bypassing normal cadence) |
| `ThresholdCrossed` | A Health/Anomaly value crosses DEGRADED or CRITICAL | Executive (immediate), Dashboard (immediate) |
| `IntentStatusChanged` | Any Intent transitions state (Section 7.2) | Constraint-check, Actuation translation, History, Dashboard |
| `ConstraintViolationAttempted` | Constraint-check rejects/modifies an Intent | History (always), Dashboard, Executive (informs next proposal) |
| `TaskCompleted` / `TaskFailed` | Mission sub-graph node status change | Executive, Dashboard, downstream mission logic |
| `LinkStatusChanged` | Comms link to ground or to flight controller changes state | Executive, Dashboard, History |

**9.2 Event propagation rule**

Every event is published once, to a single event bus, and every interested consumer subscribes independently — no consumer polls another consumer for events, and no event is ever directly routed point-to-point. This is what allows new consumers (a new dashboard view, a new logging tap, a future fleet-coordination layer) to be added by subscribing to existing event classes without altering anything about how those events are produced.

**9.3 Priority interrupt behavior**

`ConfidenceCollapsed` and `ThresholdCrossed` events carry a `priority: IMMEDIATE` flag that preempts the Executive's normal fixed-cadence evaluation loop — the Executive doesn't wait for its next scheduled cycle to react to these two event classes specifically, because they represent exactly the situations (Section 8.2, branches 1–2) where delay itself is the primary risk.

**9.4 Event/state duality**

Every event is, in effect, a notification that a piece of state has changed — the World Model remains the source of truth (state), while events are purely the *notification layer* on top of it (Section 2's "belief never skips fusion" rule still applies: an event never carries a value that bypasses the World Model — a consumer receiving `FactUpdated` still reads the current value from the World Model, it doesn't trust a value embedded in the event itself, which prevents event-ordering races from producing an inconsistent picture).

## 10. How the Dashboard Gets Updated

The ground/operator dashboard is a **pure consumer** — it has no write path into the World Model, Intent Model, or Constraint Snapshot except through the same Operator Command → Executive → Constraint-check path that any other Intent proposal follows (Section 7, Section 8.3). Its information flow:

```
World Model FactUpdated events (filtered/summarized subset)
Intent Model IntentStatusChanged events
Executive Decision Trace (justification references, resolved
   into readable form)
History query results (on-demand, for playback/review)
        │
        ▼
DASHBOARD PRESENTATION LAYER
  - Current Self-State summary (position, energy, health)
  - Current Environment/Traffic summary (near-field only,
    not full point-cloud fidelity — bandwidth-appropriate)
  - Current Intent + its justification (plain-language,
    generated from the Decision Trace)
  - Alert stream (ThresholdCrossed, ConfidenceCollapsed,
    ConstraintViolationAttempted events)
        │
        ▼
OPERATOR COMMAND (if issued)
  → treated as a PROPOSED Intent, same lifecycle as any other
    (Section 7.2) — subject to constraint-check like every
    other Intent, never a privileged bypass
```

**10.1 Bandwidth-aware information degradation**

Because the ground link may be constrained, the dashboard subscribes to a *summarized* event/fact stream rather than the full-fidelity World Model — e.g., it receives `ObstacleTrack` counts and closest-approach summaries rather than the full point cloud, and receives `SubsystemHealth` status enums rather than raw sensor time-series. Full-fidelity data remains available on the aircraft (and in History) for post-flight review even when it was never fully downlinked in real time.

**10.2 Link-loss behavior**

On `LinkStatusChanged → LOST`, the dashboard's last-known state is explicitly flagged stale (mirroring the World Model's own staleness rule from Section 3.1) rather than silently continuing to display it as current — the same freshness discipline applied inside the aircraft is applied to what the operator sees.

## 11. How Every AI Module Reads and Writes Information

This section states, generically, the information contract that *every* learned/AI component in the system obeys — regardless of what specific inference it performs (perception classification, anomaly detection, route optimization).

**11.1 The universal AI information contract**

| | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Reads** | An AI component may read any Evidence or Belief-tier information relevant to its function (Section 1) — raw or fused sensor data, current World Model facts. |
| **Writes** | An AI component may write **only** Belief-tier facts (with mandatory confidence value) or Advisory-tier proposals (Anomaly flags, Optimization proposals). |
| **Never writes** | Intent (Section 7), Constraint Snapshot, or History directly. An AI component cannot cause an Intent to become ACTIVE, cannot alter a constraint, and cannot edit the record. |
| **Provenance** | Every fact or advisory an AI component produces is tagged with `modelId` + `modelVersion` in its provenance field — the same provenance mechanism every other evidence source uses (Section 4), so the Executive and Explainability trace treat a model's output exactly like any other confidence-scored input, no different in kind from a sensor. |

**11.2 Why this contract is drawn where it is**

- **Perception models** sit entirely inside the Evidence→Belief transformation (Stage 2, Section 5) — their output is a `ObstacleTrack` or `TerrainProfile` fact, confidence-scored like any sensor fusion output. They never touch Intent.
- **Anomaly detection models** read Belief-tier World Model facts and write `AnomalyRecord` objects — a Belief-tier object, not a decision. The Executive decides what to do about an anomaly (Section 8.2, branches 2–3); the model only asserts that one exists, with what confidence.
- **Optimization models** read Mission + Self-State + Weather and write `OptimizationProposal`-class advisories — structurally identical to a PROPOSED Intent in shape, but explicitly tagged as originating from an advisory source, so the Executive (not the model) decides whether to adopt it, and adoption still runs through the ordinary Intent lifecycle (constraint-check, priority tier, justification trace).
- **Explanation generation** reads only History/Decision Trace information (already-resolved World Model facts + justification references) and writes nothing back into World Model, Intent, or Constraint — it is a pure read-and-render process, structurally incapable of influencing a future decision, which is what keeps "the system explaining itself" from ever becoming "the system's explanation shaping its next belief."

**11.3 The single invariant that makes this whole document coherent**

Every rule above — sensors updating the World Model, decisions reading only from it, Intent staying separate from Belief, AI components writing only Belief/Advisory tiers, the dashboard being a pure consumer — collapses into one sentence:

**Information only ever becomes more certain through fusion, only ever becomes action through the Executive, and is never allowed to skip either step.**
