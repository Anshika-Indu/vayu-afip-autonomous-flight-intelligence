02 — Why it doesn't twist itself apart

Counter-rotation cancels the moment

Tilting a spinning rotor is a gyroscopic act: any rotor forced to change its spin-axis direction reacts with a torque perpendicular to both the spin and the tilt. Tilt one duct on its own and the airframe would twist around its boom.

The fix is to spin the left and right ducts in opposite directions. Tilt them at the same rate and their gyroscopic reaction torques point opposite ways too — the net moment on the airframe cancels to zero at every instant, which is why the status line reads "neutralized" through the whole transition.

Cancelling the net torque doesn't remove the stress on each spar though — each duct mount still has to resist its own reaction torque individually. That's the spar torsional shear reading: it spikes mid-transition, when the tilt rate itself peaks, and it's the number that actually limits how fast an engineer would let this transition happen.

τ_gyro = K · Ω_tilt
K — rotor inertia × spin rate (scales with payload)
Ω_tilt — rate of change of tilt angle, dθ/dt

τ_CW = +τ_gyro  ·  τ_CCW = −τ_gyro
Σ τ_net = τ_CW + τ_CCW = 0 (always)

spar shear ≈ |τ_gyro| — peaks when Ω_tilt peaks,
i.e. mid-transition, not at the tilt extremes.
